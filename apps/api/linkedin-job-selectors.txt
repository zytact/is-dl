LinkedIn hashes its class names, so every hook below is an attribute, an id-keyed
element or a link. <id> is the job id, which the search URL carries as currentJobId.

Job card
div[role="button"][componentkey^="job-card-component-ref-"]  (the key ends in the job id)

Company name (job card)
the card's second <p>. The first is the title and the third the location. No attribute marks it.

Next page
[data-testid="pagination-controls-next-button-visible"]

Job title (detail pane)
a[href*="/jobs/view/<id>/"]

Top card (detail pane)
the title's nearest ancestor that contains a <figure>, the company logo, and does
not contain #JobDetails_AboutTheJob_<id>

Company name
figure + p in the top card. A company with a LinkedIn page also gets an
[aria-label="Company, <name>."] wrapper. One without a page gets plain text.

Company URL
a[href*="/company/"] inside the company name, absent for a company without a LinkedIn page

Location and posted time
the first <p> in the top card containing " · ", for example "India · 12 hours ago · 79 people clicked apply"

Job type, workplace and pay pills
the pill row is the first sibling after the top card with a child that holds an
a[href*="currentJobId=<id>"] link. Each pill is one such link in its own child.
Other links back to the job, such as "I'm interested" under "About the company",
sit outside that row.

Job description
#JobDetails_AboutTheJob_<id> [data-testid="expandable-text-box"]

Search filters
Location, remote, experience level and job type are read from the query text.
Only f_TPR (posted within) and start (paging) still work as URL parameters.
